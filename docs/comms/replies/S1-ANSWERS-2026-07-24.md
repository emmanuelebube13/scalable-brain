## Q2 — Gatekeeper promotion and the real thresholds

### (a) Why `_default_promote` never promotes the gatekeeper

**By construction, not by intent. It is a missing wire, not a deliberate decoupling.**

- `orchestrator._gatekeeper_metrics()` calls `gatekeeper.train.run(dry_run=True)` for the
  sole purpose of harvesting an uplift number to feed the `oos_uplift_ok` gate. Dry-run
  writes `models/proposed_champion_*` and by design never touches the live champion.
- `orchestrator._default_promote()` calls `serialize.publish()`, whose `SOURCES` are
  `hmm_model.joblib`, `strategy_weights.json`, `regime_strategy_map.json`. **The gatekeeper
  is not in that bundle.**
- `serializer/publish_gatekeeper.py` is a separate manual entry point. Nothing in the
  orchestrator calls it.

So each cycle trains a gatekeeper, uses its uplift to authorise a promote, then discards
it. FIX-S1-009 made the orchestrator the single governed writer of the champion bundle;
the gatekeeper's own promotion path was left manual and never reconnected.

### (b) The real live thresholds

From `models/champion_manifest.json` (created 2026-07-05, `dry_run: false`):

```
High-Vol      0.45
Ranging       0.60
Trending-Down 0.60
Trending-Up   0.50
fallback      0.60
```

### (c) Does it hit the [5%, 60%] band? — measured, not inferred

We loaded the live champion and preprocessor and scored all 134,520 trades.

**Your suspicion was directionally right but not literally right.** Scores are not all
crushed into 0.19–0.25 — the range is **0.2301 to 0.8114**, median **0.4766**. But the
distribution is compressed and sits mostly *below* the thresholds:

```
percentiles [0, 1, 5, 25, 50, 75, 95, 99, 100]
   0.2301  0.2713  0.3014  0.4295  0.4766  0.5948  0.6046  0.6128  0.8114
```

| | Live champion (07-05) | Proposed (07-19) |
|---|---|---|
| Manifest **claims** OOS approval | 0.3379 | 0.2891 |
| **Actual** approval of the shipped model | **0.1723** | 0.3416 |

**Per-regime approval of the live champion:**

| Regime | n | Threshold | Median score | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ranging | 82,543 | 0.60 | 0.4770 | **0.1168** |
| Trending-Down | 27,998 | 0.60 | 0.4453 | **0.1108** |
| Trending-Up | 18,449 | 0.50 | 0.4774 | 0.4219 |
| High-Vol | 5,530 | 0.45 | 0.4453 | 0.4803 |

**Ranging holds ~95% of live capital and gets an 11.7% approval rate.** The regime where
the money is, is the regime where the gate is tightest.

**Root cause — a calibration mismatch:**

`train.py:288` ships `dynamic_thresholds = wf["thresholds"]`, which is `last_thresholds` —
the threshold map calibrated on the **final walk-forward fold's validation split**. But
`train.py:286-287` then refits the shipped model on the **entire frame**. Thresholds
calibrated against fold-model A are shipped attached to full-refit model B, whose score
distribution is different. That is the whole gap between the advertised 33.79% and the
actual 17.23%.

**Consequence for the business rule:** `is_degenerate()` is applied to the walk-forward
approval rate (0.3379), never to the shipped artifact's real rate (0.1723). **The
[5%, 60%] turnover band is not enforced against the thing that ships.** In this instance
17.2% still lands inside the band, so nothing is degenerate today — but the guard is not
actually guarding the artifact, and the manifest figure you consume is roughly 2× reality.

**Before you promote the 07-19 model, know this:** it would move approval from 17.2% to
34.2%, roughly doubling the trades let through. That is a material change in trading
behaviour, not a routine model refresh. It should be a deliberate, staged decision.

## Q3 — Packaging, and what `beats_incumbent` actually measures

### (a) Who writes the top-level `latest.json`?

**Nobody in System 1. There is no code in this repository that produces it.** Verified by
grep across `src/`. The only `latest.json` writers here are
`system1/latest.json` (serializer), `models/gatekeeper/latest.json`
(publish_gatekeeper), and `system1/analytics/latest.json` (analytics).

The top-level manifest is the 7-artifact set your EXEC-001 downloader spec expects
(`docs/implementation-roadmap/system-2-execution-engine/tasks/01-model-downloader-and-validator.md`).
Its current content:

```
model_set_id : 2026-07-01T12-56-32Z_gk-656f09e2
published_at : 2026-07-10T00:00:00Z        <- round timestamp; hand-authored
artifacts    : hmm_model.joblib            -> 2026-07-01T12-56-32Z/...
               regime_strategy_map.json    -> 2026-07-01T12-56-32Z/...
               strategy_weights.json       -> 2026-07-01T12-56-32Z/...
               model_metadata.json         -> 2026-07-01T12-56-32Z/...
               champion_model.pkl          -> models/gatekeeper/2026-07-05T17-43-09Z-.../
               champion_preprocessor.pkl   -> models/gatekeeper/2026-07-05T17-43-09Z-.../
               champion_manifest.json      -> models/gatekeeper/2026-07-05T17-43-09Z-.../
```

Meanwhile `system1/latest.json` points at `2026-07-19T00-28-32Z-87628c72`.

**So the manifest S2 is specified to read is three weeks and two promotes stale.** Any
consumer following the spec is loading the July 1 model set. Note the shape of this
failure: S1's verify-before-flip publish contract is intact and working, and it is being
defeated by a hand-maintained pointer sitting *above* it.

**Should S1 own it?** That is your scope decision, not mine to make. The case for yes:
S1 is the only producer of both halves of that manifest, and the packaging step needs the
same verify-then-flip discipline the two lower pointers already have. Say the word and it
becomes a governed step in the orchestrator, written only after both underlying pointers
have flipped.

### (b) Does `beats_incumbent` measure the granularity you trade?

`_default_pipeline` computes `regime_accuracy = min(accs)` across **all** granularities,
D1 included. It measures whichever is *worst*.

On the 2026-07-19 run: D1 0.887, H4 0.860, H1 0.861 → min = 0.8603, which came from **H4**
— a granularity you do trade. That was incidental. D1 is in the pool, and historical D1
K-Means runs scored as low as 0.545. A bad D1 run would block a promote on a granularity
carrying zero capital, and a good D1 run can mask a degraded H1.

### (c) The finding you did not ask for but need

**On the 2026-07-19 promote, `beats_incumbent` compared nothing.** The retrain log records:

```json
"incumbent": {},
"gates": { "beats_incumbent": true, ... }
```

It passed because there was nothing to compare against, per the documented first-publish
fail-open policy — not because the candidate beat anything.

Cause, confirmed against the bucket: the serializer's `MODEL_PREFIX = "system1"` scoping
means `_incumbent()` reads `system1/latest.json`. The 2026-07-01 bundle was published at
the **bucket root** (`2026-07-01T12-56-32Z/`). Listing confirms it: root-level prefixes
include `2026-07-01T12-56-32Z`, while `system1/` contains only the 07-19 bundle.

The prefix migration orphaned the old pointer, so at 2026-07-19T00:00 the new pointer did
not yet exist, `_incumbent()` returned `{}`, and the regression gate silently reset. It
will bind normally from the next promote onward — but the 07-19 model went live without
ever being compared to its predecessor.

## Q4 — Will S1 own and publish `risk/strategy_stats` on a cadence?

**This is a scope decision for the operator, not something S1's code can answer.** Here is
the factual position so the decision can be made cleanly:

- **No S1 code produces `risk/strategy_stats`.** No spec for it exists in this repository —
  grep found nothing in `othersystemcommunication/` or `src/`.
- The `risk/` prefix **does** exist in the bucket. That is your hand-seeding; S1 has never
  written to it.

**What S1 does publish today**, on an automatic cadence, is the S1-EXPORT-002 analytics
bundle at `system1/analytics/<version>/`:

- `strategy_catalog.json`
- `trade_returns.json` — OOS per-trade R-multiple series per qualified cell
- `frequency_stats.json` — trade frequency + regime occupancy

Refreshed by the orchestrator after every successful promote, under the same contract as
the model bundle: immutable versioned prefix, SHA256 verify, pointer flipped last, and a
failure there can never roll back the promote. Latest version:
`2026-07-19T00-28-39Z-6a602ce2`.

**Recommendation if you decide yes:** host it in the existing analytics publisher rather
than as a new path. It already has the cadence, the verify-then-flip discipline, and the
promote-decoupled failure semantics. A second publisher would be a second thing to keep
coherent, and Q3 shows what that costs.

## Unprompted finding: High-Vol is empty for a *gate* reason, not an *edge* reason

You have no coverage in volatile markets. The cause is not that the strategy fails there.

Strategy 10 attribution, current qualification run `b678e2de`:

| Regime | Gran | n | Win rate | PF | Sharpe | MaxDD | Recovery | OOS months | low_conf |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High-Vol** | **H1** | **21** | **0.714** | **2.23** | **0.90** | **0.010** | **7.34** | **47.8** | **False** |
| Ranging | H1 | 335 | 0.731 | 2.94 | 3.80 | 0.046 | 75.83 | 83.8 | False |
| Trending-Down | H1 | 117 | 0.769 | 3.24 | 2.58 | 0.030 | 26.05 | 77.8 | False |
| Trending-Up | H1 | 79 | 0.646 | 1.84 | 1.01 | 0.040 | 5.91 | 83.8 | False |

High-Vol @ H1 clears **five of six gates** — PF 2.23 ≥ 1.5, Sharpe 0.90 ≥ 0.8,
MaxDD 1.0% ≤ 25%, WinRate 71.4% ≥ 40%, Recovery 7.34 ≥ 3.0 — and is **not** flagged
low-confidence. It is excluded solely by `oos_months` **47.8 < 60**.

So the portfolio's uncovered exposure in volatile conditions is produced by a calendar
coverage threshold, not by an absence of demonstrated edge. Whether 60 months is the right
number is a policy question worth putting on the table explicitly — it is currently
costing you an entire regime's coverage.
