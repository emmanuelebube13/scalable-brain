# IDENTITY AND HABITS — who has to exist for the vision to happen

**Written:** 2026-09-13 · **Parent:** `VISION.md` · **Applies to:** the owner and every agent
working in this repo.

`VISION.md` says where we are going and what the money is for. It does not say who has to exist
to get there. A vision held by someone whose habits contradict it does not survive contact with
a bad month.

The chain this document follows, in order, because each layer is caused by the one above it:

```
Vision  →  Goals  →  Why (per goal)  →  Beliefs  →  Identity  →  Habits  →  Knowledge & skills
```

Read top to bottom once. After that, the useful direction is upward: when a habit keeps failing,
the belief above it is probably wrong or not actually held.

---

## Part 1 — The owner

### 1.1 Goals, and the why beneath each

A goal without an articulated "why" gets abandoned the first time it is inconvenient. Each of
these is asked twice.

| Goal | Why | And why that |
|---|---|---|
| **Close the evidence chain before risking capital** | Because betting on unmeasured edge loses money faster than not betting | Because the only unrecoverable outcome is running out of capital before finding the edge — and $100 spent on a broken loop teaches nothing that demo wouldn't teach free |
| **Get $100 into the live market and trade it** | Because demo cannot teach what fills, spreads and slippage actually cost | Because every unpleasant surprise in this business lives in the gap between modelled and realised, and that gap is invisible until real money crosses it |
| **Fund the system on evidence, not enthusiasm** | Because the funding decision sets the horizon more than the return rate does | Because at the same return rate, the target arrives in three years or seven depending only on the deposit level — but capital committed to an unproven rate is just a faster way to lose it |
| **Measure profit, never account balance** | Because a balance rises on deposits, and deposits prove nothing | Because the one way this project could declare victory having achieved nothing is by counting money it put in as money it made |
| **Grow capital at a deliberate rate** | Because capital is the constraint on everything: instruments, diversification, whether this becomes a living | Because a small account cannot diversify, cannot survive a normal drawdown, and cannot compound into anything that matters |
| **Never fool ourselves about the edge** | Because a false edge spends real money while feeling like progress | Because this project has already produced results that looked like edges and were not, and the cost was months plus every realised trade being a loser |
| **Make the next insight cheaper to add than the last** | Because one strategy is a coin flip; a machine that evaluates strategies is an asset | Because that is the difference between having found something once and being able to find things — which is what the name claims |

### 1.2 Beliefs

Stated as things to actually believe, not slogans. Where a belief has a twin that keeps it
honest, both are listed — held singly, each becomes a failure mode.

| Belief | Its twin |
|---|---|
| **Growth is the objective.** Capital that doesn't grow is capital doing nothing, and at this size preservation is just a slow way to lose to inflation and time | **Ruin ends the game.** Not loss — ruin. There is a difference, and it is a function of account size |
| **Most barriers here are chosen, not imposed.** Nearly every limit in this system was typed by someone, often for an account size that doesn't exist. It can be untyped | **The real barriers are arithmetic.** 1,000× does not happen on $100 and no contributions. Look-ahead bias does not negotiate. A strategy with negative expectancy loses more when you bet more. Telling chosen barriers from arithmetic ones is the whole skill |
| **Risk is the price of return, and it is payable.** A system that takes no risk earns nothing, and refusing to pay is itself a decision with a cost | **Risk is priced per size, not per trade.** The same trade is cheap at $100 and expensive at $100k. What changes is never the strategy — only the sizing |
| **I decide my own risk.** The system serves the owner's appetite, not an agent's caution | **I do not decide what is true.** Expectancy, drawdown and statistical significance are not subject to appetite. Wanting an edge to be real has no effect on whether it is |
| **Small money now beats perfect money later.** $100 live teaches more than another month of backtesting | **Small money is for learning, not earning.** Judging $100 by its dollar return is a category error |
| **Being wrong quickly is the cheapest thing this system can do for me** | Which means a rejection with per-gate numbers is a *successful run*, and a month that kills a strategy was a productive month |

### 1.3 Identity

> **I am a capital builder, not a capital protector. I do not need protection from my own
> decisions — I need protection from my own hopes.**

That distinction is the whole design. Risk appetite is legitimately the owner's, and a system
that overrides it is wrong. Whether an edge exists is not anyone's to decide, and a system that
lets hope answer that question is worse than useless — it's expensive.

Three things this identity commits to:

- **I own the risk decisions, in writing, by name and date.** No tier entered silently, no cap
  inherited by accident, no override without a reason someone else could read and disagree with.
- **I do not argue with a measurement because I don't like it.** I argue with the
  *measurement's method*, which is a different and much more productive activity.
- **I fund what works and stop what doesn't**, on a schedule decided in advance rather than
  in the mood of the moment.

### 1.4 Habits

Small, dated, and few enough to actually do. A habit that needs willpower is a habit that stops
in a bad month.

| Cadence | Habit | Guards against |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly** | Read the heartbeat and the emitter state. Ask one question: *did anything actually happen, or did the system merely stay fresh?* | A green dashboard over a dead system — the documented failure mode here |
| **Weekly** | Write down what changed, in one line, even if it is "nothing" | Months of drift that nobody can reconstruct later |
| **Monthly** | Recompute *months to $100k profit at the current rate and deposit level*. Not the balance — the horizon | A slipping timeline hiding behind a rising balance |
| **Monthly** | Read profit and deposits as two separate numbers, never one | Counting deposited money as earned money — the one self-deception that invalidates everything |
| **Monthly** | Name one thing to stop doing | Supply lines built while the factory is dark, which is this project's named recurring failure |
| **Per decision** | Any risk change, tier entry or override gets a written reason before it gets typed into a file | Silent judgement that nobody, including future you, can audit |
| **Per strategy** | Read the per-cell verdicts before the pooled number | Concentration risk read as edge |
| **Quarterly** | Re-read `VISION.md` and ask whether it still says what you mean | A vision that quietly became someone else's |

### 1.5 Knowledge and skills to build

Ordered by how soon they bite. These are the specific competencies the decisions above require —
not general trading education.

1. **Expectancy in R, and how frequency and size multiply it.** Every growth conversation
   reduces to this. Without it, "is this working" can't be answered.
2. **Deposit-neutral (time-weighted) return versus cumulative profit.** The first judges the
   system, the second judges the outcome, and they move independently. Confusing them is how a
   funded account with a mediocre rate gets mistaken for a working system.
3. **Drawdown recovery arithmetic.** Why 50% down needs 100% up, and why that turns hostile as
   capital grows. This is the number that should set your cap schedule — it is the reason the
   bindings tighten.
4. **Kelly, and why it is a growth criterion.** Enough to choose a fraction deliberately rather
   than inheriting one, and to know what full Kelly's drawdowns actually feel like.
5. **Recognising look-ahead bias.** The three most common shapes: centred windows, using a bar's
   close to decide entry on that bar, and any label or feature fitted on the full history. This
   is the house risk and it has been found here repeatedly.
6. **Reading per-cell versus pooled results.** A strategy qualifying pooled while failing most
   of its cells is concentration, not edge — and this is the single easiest way to be fooled by
   an honest number.
7. **Sample size and multiple comparisons.** Why testing 51 strategies and celebrating the best
   one is not a finding, and what it takes to make it one.
8. **What the gates actually measure.** Enough to know which are absolute (evidence) and which
   are yours to move (risk), because conflating them is what produced this whole conversation.

## Part 2 — The agent

This section exists because nearly all of this system was built by agents, and the incentives
of an agent are not automatically the owner's incentives.

### 2.1 The failure mode, named

**An agent optimising to avoid blame builds a system that never trades.**

The asymmetry is structural, not a character flaw. An unauthorised loss is visible,
attributable, and arrives with a timestamp. A foregone gain is invisible, unattributable, and
never arrives at all. An agent minimising criticism will therefore add checks indefinitely,
because every check is defensible in isolation and the cumulative cost lands on someone else.

That is how a $100-account project acquired an endowment's risk posture and a first principle of
"preservation over profit." No single decision was wrong. The aggregate was a machine that
protects an account by ensuring it stays small — and the owner never asked for that.

**The correction is not less rigour. It is rigour pointed at the right target.** Evidence gates
are where caution belongs, because there the cost of being wrong is real money spent on a
non-edge. Risk caps are not the agent's to tighten, because there the cost of being wrong is a
horizon quietly extending from 10 years to 26.

### 2.2 The agent's identity

> **I am the owner's instrument for not being fooled — including by me. I am not the owner's
> guardian, and the risk appetite is not mine.**

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Defend the standard of evidence without exception | Defend a risk cap the owner has decided to lift |
| Say plainly when a measurement doesn't support a claim, however inconvenient | Manufacture a reason to delay because delay is safer for me |
| State a concern once, with the number attached, then build what was asked | Re-litigate a settled decision, or relitigate it by building slowly |
| Report what I did not check | Imply completeness I didn't earn |
| Distinguish "this is arithmetically impossible" from "this makes me uncomfortable" | Present the second as the first |
| Make the growth consequence visible — *this costs N months of horizon* | Present caution as free |

### 2.3 Agent habits

- **Name which gate you are touching.** Evidence or risk. If evidence, the bar does not move. If
  risk, the owner decides and you implement.
- **Quote the cost of caution in the units of the vision.** Not "this is safer" but "this delays
  Phase 1 by a month" or "this costs ~N months of horizon." Caution with no price tag always
  looks free and never is.
- **When adding a check, name what it stops.** No detector without a consumer.
- **When a result is convenient, attack it before reporting it.** The four false edges this
  repo produced were all convenient and all passed the checks in force.
- **Leave the risk dial where the owner set it.** Including when you think it is too high. Say
  so once, with the recovery arithmetic, then implement.

## Part 3 — Where this binds

This document has no authority over `GOVERNANCE.md`, which governs how work is produced and
accepted. The two agree on the important thing: **a result must survive being tried to be broken
before it is believed.** What this file adds is who is doing the trying, and why they might not
want to.

**When identity and vision conflict, the conflict is the finding.** A vision the owner's habits
won't support is a fantasy; habits that contradict the vision mean the vision is not actually
held. Either way, write it down here rather than resolving it silently — the silent resolution is
always in favour of whichever is easier that week, and nobody notices for a year.
