## ISS-2026-09-05-4 — `attribute.py` now requires an engine version; the value is unset

**Severity:** blocking for retrain (deliberately)

Applied in this pass (§B2 of the report): `attribute._load_trades` and `attribute.run` now
require `engine_version`, and `scheduler/orchestrator.py` reads
`attribute.AUTHORITATIVE_ENGINE_FOR_VETTING`, which is **`None`**. A retrain will raise
until it is set.

This is intentional. The two engines' `r_multiple` are not the same quantity (v2 moves 18–34%
of its stops and scales out; v1 does neither) and **no strategy runs under both**, so there is
no data-driven tie-break. The retrain cron is not installed, so nothing fires unattended.

**Owner decision required:** set the constant to `"position_engine_v2"` (the fill-timing
argument favours it, and it is 170 of the 209 cells) or to `"backtest_engine_v1"`, and record
why.

## ISS-2026-09-05-5 — `get_pip_value(metadata.pairs[0])` makes every pip quantity 100× too small on JPY pairs

**Severity:** high — a concrete code defect with a measured cost and a one-line fix.

```python
pip = float(get_pip_value(self.metadata.pairs[0]))   # smashing_forex_2.py:62
```

The pip value is resolved **once from a hard-coded first element of the pairs list** and then
reused for every pair the strategy trades. `pairs[0]` is a non-JPY major in every affected
file, so on USD_JPY the strategy uses 0.0001 where it should use 0.01.

**13 research strategies use this idiom in code** (counted by AST, not grep — a regex also
matches the warning docstring in `double_bottom_measured_move.py`, which is in fact one of
the *correct* ones). It only causes harm where a pip-denominated term
is *load-bearing on the stop* (a cap, a floor, or the stop itself). Measured, in stop ÷ ATR:

| strategy | non-JPY | USD_JPY | ratio | mean R non-JPY | mean R USD_JPY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `riding_trend_retracement` | 3.083 | 0.065 | **0.021** | +0.218 | **−4.585** |
| `smashing_forex_2` | 1.786 | 0.075 | **0.042** | −0.055 | **−1.065** |
| `three_candle_swing_reversal` | 0.715 | 0.335 | 0.468 | +0.057 | **−1.153** |
| `liquidity_sweep_ob` | 0.628 | 0.510 | 0.813 | −0.744 | −1.271 |
| the other constant-pip strategies with JPY trades | — | — | 0.87–1.02 | — | — |
| the 10 correct per-pair strategies | — | — | 0.82–1.12 | — | — |

Mechanism, from source: `smashing_forex_2.py:88-90` takes
`dist = min(dist_ema, STOP_CAP_PIPS * pip)`, so the 100×-too-small cap wins;
`riding_trend_retracement.py:167-171` binds the stop with a pip-denominated `max(...)`;
`three_candle_swing_reversal.py:93` likewise. By contrast `liquidity_grab_fade.py:190` uses
`grab_extreme - 4.0 * pip`, a small buffer on a price-derived level — ratio 0.968, immaterial.

`riding_trend_retracement`'s mean of −4.585 R is a consequence, not a separate mystery: with
a 47×-too-tight stop the R denominator is tiny, so ordinary adverse moves become huge R
multiples.

**The engine is not at fault.** v1 lands on exactly 1.500 × ATR for USD_JPY, identical to
every other pair, and the measured exit slippage is 0.5 pips on USD_JPY as everywhere.

**The underlying cause is a contract gap, not 13 independent slips.**
`StrategyV2.generate_orders(frames)` is never given the pair it is running on
(`contract_v2.py`), so a strategy that needs a pip size has to find it somewhere else.
`double_bottom_measured_move.py:47-53` names the hazard verbatim — *"`generate_orders`
receives frames only, never the pair, so `get_pip_value(metadata.pairs[0])` would silently
apply the EUR_USD pip (0.0001) to USD_JPY trades too"* — and three strategies
(`amazing_crossover`, `holy_grail_pullback`, `double_bottom_measured_move`) wrote
`_pip_size_from_price` to work around it. The hazard was documented before this audit; what
was missing was any measurement of who had fallen into it.

**Fix, two levels:**
1. Per file — resolve the pip per pair inside the loop, or adopt the existing
   `_pip_size_from_price` helper.
2. Structurally — pass the pair to `generate_orders` so the gap cannot be re-entered. That is
   a v2 contract change and belongs to whoever owns `contract_v2.py`.

Requires a `forex-strategist` and `leakage-hunter` pass (`src/layer0/CLAUDE.md`) and a
`persist_all` rebuild. Tracked as O-28.

**Not fixed in this pass**: the engine-validation spec's Part B is scoped to the engine, and
editing four strategies re-derives outcomes → attribution → map → weights.
