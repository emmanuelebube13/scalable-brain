## 2. 🔴 SECOND FINDING — your live entries are not this strategy's entries

`Range_Stochastic_Divergence` is a **mean-reversion oscillator**
(`src/layer0/strategies/strategieStaged/range_stochastic.py:136-165`): it buys %K crossing up out
of oversold and sells %K crossing down out of overbought. **Direction comes from the instrument's
own oscillator. The regime label plays no part in it.**

Here is the backtest's own direction mix for strategy 10 @H1, joined to the causal regime:

| regime | direction | n | win rate | mean R |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **Trending-Down** | **long** | **61** | **0.770** | **+0.487** |
| Trending-Down | short | 87 | 0.736 | +0.441 |
| Ranging | long | 245 | 0.718 | +0.424 |
| Ranging | short | 242 | 0.736 | +0.466 |

**In a downtrend this strategy goes LONG 41% of the time — and those longs win 77%.** It is a
mean-reversion strategy; buying the dip in a downtrend is where much of its edge lives.

Your live book: **13 of 13 Trending-Down trades were SHORT.** Under the backtest's own direction
mix (P(short) = 0.588) that is **p = 0.001 — about 1 in 999.**

And the entry timestamps cluster:

```
2026-07-15T13:00   EUR_USD, GBP_USD, AUD_USD
2026-07-19T22:00   EUR_USD, GBP_USD, AUD_USD
2026-07-27T03:00   EUR_USD, GBP_USD, AUD_USD, USD_CAD
```

Three or four instruments entering on the **identical H1 bar**. Independent per-instrument
stochastic crossings do not synchronise like that. A regime/batch trigger does.

Your §9 §3 says:

**Please re-open F-405.** It is the best candidate I have for the 7.7% win rate, and it is testable
